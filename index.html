The problem with sampling

Sampling was a concession to arithmetic, not a method anyone preferred.

Internal audit tests a sample because a person cannot foot four million journal lines. The sample is defensible and the exception it misses is still an exception. Continuous auditing removes the concession: the computer tests the population.

A sample of 40

Seven tested · one exception, untested

Coverage is a percentage of a percentage. The finding that matters is the one outside the sample, and nothing in the working paper says it was there.

The whole population

Forty tested · the exception is found

The same control, applied to every row, every night. The exception is not a matter of luck, and the coverage statement in your audit file is simply all of it.

How it works

Four stages, running unattended between close of business and morning.

The sequence is ordinary data engineering built for an audit standard of evidence: every copy reconciled to the source by count and by sum before a single test runs.

Stage 1

Connect

Read-only credentials against Oracle, SQL Server, PostgreSQL or MySQL — your core banking, ERP, HR and lending systems. Views are enough; base tables are not required.

Stage 2

Extract

Incremental extraction on a bookmark, so tonight reads only what changed. A dropped connection resumes where it stopped and never skips a posting.

Stage 3

Test

Twenty-nine audit tests over the full population — duplicates, segregation of duties, Benford's Law, outliers, aging, gaps — plus your own SQL and Python.

Stage 4

Resolve

Findings land in exception tables your team works through: assign, comment, close, raise to Jira or ServiceNow. Every collection is kept, so a trend is real.

Test library

The tests an audit manual already asks for, written once and run every night.

Each is a dataset-driven function over the extracted population, not a stored report. Point one at a table, set its thresholds, and it runs on the schedule you give it.

Deployment and data protection

It installs inside your perimeter. Your data never leaves it.

Sources it reads

Oracle SQL Server PostgreSQL MySQL Temenos T24 Dynamics 365 Stripe Salesforce NetSuite HubSpot Zendesk CSV & Excel

Connections are read-only by design and their credentials are held in HashiCorp Vault. A connection belongs to a team, and a team sees only what it owns.

01

On your servers, or none at all

Windows or Linux, with or without Docker. An installation that cannot reach the internet works exactly the same — the licence is a signed file, not a call home.

02

A tamper-evident audit trail

Every action is journalled to an append-only chain on disk, so the log can be shipped somewhere DataBridge cannot reach and an edit to it shows.

03

Controls a bank's IT will ask about

Team-scoped access on every route, optional second factor, sign-in through your identity provider, and scheduled backups that are verified restorable.

04

Nothing stops quietly

A job that cannot run fails loudly, records why, and emails whoever watches the overnight controls. A control that stops without saying so is the failure this product exists to catch.
